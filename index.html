A city between mountains and history

Welcome to Tbilisi

Georgia’s capital is a place of winding old streets, welcoming tables, and views that stay with you.

A city with many stories

Tbilisi sits in the valley of the Mtkvari River, surrounded by hills. Its name is connected to the warm sulfur springs that have drawn visitors for centuries. In the Old Town, wooden balconies lean over narrow lanes, while colorful courtyards hide behind carved doors. A short walk can take you from ancient churches to lively cafés and modern galleries.

Places to explore

The city is easy to explore slowly. Ride the cable car toward Narikala Fortress for a wide view across the rooftops, then walk down toward the bathhouses in Abanotubani. Cross the river to see the modern Bridge of Peace, or spend an afternoon browsing the stalls and cafés around Dry Bridge Market. Each neighborhood has its own pace and character.

  • Walk through the lanes of the Old Town
  • See the city from Narikala Fortress
  • Visit the sulfur baths of Abanotubani

Good food and warm company

Food is an important part of Georgian hospitality. Try khachapuri, a warm bread filled with cheese, or khinkali, dumplings traditionally eaten by hand. A meal often lasts a long time because people make room for conversation, stories, and one more plate. Local bakeries, family restaurants, and small wine bars make it easy to discover something new around every corner.

Why I would return

Tbilisi feels both historic and full of energy. You can spend the morning looking at old architecture, take a quiet break beside the river, and finish the day listening to music in a busy square. The city rewards curiosity: turn down a side street, speak with a shopkeeper, or try a dish you have never tasted. Its mix of scenery, culture, and kindness makes Tbilisi a place I would love to visit again.
